32 things to do in Ljubljana, Slovenia’s fairytale capital

Here are some of my favourite things to do in Ljubljana, Slovenia. I’ve been very excited to write this guide as Ljubljana is my favourite city in Europe. I recently spent 10 days there so I could do loads of research. To me, it’s just the perfect city: affordable, great restaurants, unusual architecture, compact and walkable size, stunningly beautiful. It ticks every box.

The city feels like a storybook setting: bridges cross the Ljubljanica river, a castle watches from its hill, and willow-shaded cobbled banks are lined with bars and restaurants. It is relaxed, spotless and full of greenery, with one of the EU’s largest car-free areas and a Nordic feel at far lower prices than Copenhagen. The personal recommendations follow below.

Things to do in Ljubljana, Slovenia

1. Learn how to pronounce ‘Ljubljana’

The one thing to know before arriving: the name is said Loo-blah-na.

2. Do the free walking tour – one of the best things to do in Ljubljana on your first day there

A free walking tour is the ideal start, giving an overview, some history and a sense of direction. The two-hour tour leaves from outside the pink church at 11am year-round, with extra 3pm and 6pm departures in summer; the morning slot leaves time to revisit favourite sights later.

No booking is needed: look for the guide with the yellow umbrella and leave a tip at the end (around 10€ is suggested). The guides are lively and cover both the city’s past and its green credentials.

3. See the pink Franciscan Church

The tour’s meeting point is also the old town’s star attraction. Built between 1646 and 1660, the church gained its Baroque front in 1703–1706; that facade was painted red, the Franciscan colour, and has faded over time to the salmon pink everyone photographs.

Inside are gold, white and dark-wood details, an ornate ceiling and a saint’s remains in a glass coffin near the altar. It stays open until 8pm, so it is an easy end-of-day stop.

4. Learn about Jože Plečnik, the architect behind Ljubljana (and visit his house)

Architect Jože Plečnik shaped Ljubljana much as Gaudí shaped Barcelona, and he has become the author’s favourite architect. His work, including the Triple Bridge, the central market, the library and the riverside embankments, pairs striking looks with everyday usefulness.

His house, built for relatives who never moved in, is now a museum full of his experimental designs, seen on a guided tour or via the small downstairs exhibition. Opening times and prices are listed on the official website.

5. Get into Slovenian modern and contemporary art at the Museum of Modern Art

The museum highlights Central European artists who are less known elsewhere, such as Piran-born painter Joni Zakonjšek. It takes about an hour, sits beside Tivoli Park and has a café below for people-watching. Entry is 5€, free on the first Sunday of each month.

6. Get to know ALL the bridges

Roughly 17 bridges cross the river. Asked to widen one busy crossing, Plečnik instead added two footbridges and rows of pillars, creating the Triple Bridge. The stone-guarded Dragon Bridge recalls the legend of Jason and the Argonauts fighting a dragon here on their way to the Adriatic.

Cobblers’, Trnovo and Butchers’ bridges also carry Plečnik’s touch, and in autumn St James’ Bridge is worth a detour once its ivy turns bright red from mid-October.

7. Do some dragon-spotting

That legend made the dragon the city’s emblem, so look for it on flags, drain covers, the coat of arms, benches and plenty of souvenirs.

8. Browse some lovely independent shops

Skip the familiar chains and head for independent shops, especially on Stari Trg, where local jewellery and homewares have a Scandinavian look.

These are some of my favourite shops in Ljubljana:

  • GUD shop – quirky homewares, geometric jewellery and botanical prints; a city-print tote bought here in 2017 still looks new.
  • Extraordinary Ljubljana – ethical clothing brands, including Veja trainers.
  • Dolenjska Delights – local honey, jam, spirits, cheese and other deli gifts.

9. Drink an orange wine or two

Orange wine, first tried here in 2017, is now easier to find in the UK, but it tastes best fresh in Slovenia. Its colour is closer to mustard than orange, and the country exports very little of its wine.

10. Visit the bustling Central Market and its ‘Open Kitchen’

Spread over two squares, the market is a hub of local life, with Plečnik’s pillared halls and even a fresh-milk vending machine.

On Fridays from March to October, the ‘Open Kitchen’ brings pop-up stalls where local restaurants serve street-food versions of their dishes, plus wine and craft beer. For fresh produce, go on a Saturday morning; the covered section closes at 2pm.

11. Find the prettiest street in the old town

Križevniška Ulica is a top photo spot, with summer flowers, autumn greenery, a framed church spire, evening fairy lights and a few good restaurants.

12. See Ljubljana from the water with a river cruise

A cruise passes beneath many of the bridges. Barka Ljubljanica runs the only wooden boat, and showing a walking-tour ticket once earned a free drink. Trips cost 12€, last 50 minutes, head towards Špica where the river divides and loop back past the old town.

13. Get the funicular up to the castle and enjoy the view

Ljubljana Castle crowns the hill above the city. The one-minute funicular leaves from near the market; ride it around sunset, then walk down later.

At dusk the view takes in golden rooftops, the river and the Kamnik Alps beyond, and the grounds hold museums and exhibitions for rainy days. The funicular alone is normally 3.50€ one way, while castle entry with a return ride is 16€.

14. Have a delicious lunch at Gostilna Vida

Gostilna Vida is a vegan-friendly restaurant with street-side tables and a garden room. Good picks include the gado gado salad with tempeh, buckwheat sandwiches and soba noodles, with a matcha tiramisu tempting dessert-lovers. Salads cost about 13€ and sandwiches 8.50€.

15. Visit the Slovenian Railway Museum

Written during a 3.5-month rail trip around Europe, this pick is set in a 20th-century railway workshop filled with steam locomotives and a model railway that staff switch on for visitors.

Some trains and carriages can be boarded, a smaller building shows signals and levers, and an upstairs room displays railway art. Entry is 8€, with a ticket styled like an old train ticket.

16. Wander through Tivoli Park and spot some dogs with no tongues…

The large park has a mansion, trails, a wildlife-rich pond and Plečnik’s column-lined Jakopič Promenade, which often hosts outdoor exhibitions. The mansion houses the International Centre of Graphic Arts.

Four cast-iron dogs by sculptor Anton Dominik Fernkorn, who also made the horseman statue in Zagreb’s Jelačić Square, guard its steps, and none has a tongue. Local legend links the oversight to his tragic end; that story is untrue, though he did later become seriously ill.

17. Enjoy the bar and café culture in the evenings

Evenings are made for strolling the riverside, where bars and cafés set out glowing tables beneath willows; even in autumn it is usually mild enough to sit outside.

18. Eat some traditional Slovenian štruklji

Štruklji are filo dumplings with sweet or savoury fillings. The specialist café My Dumplings Of Slovenia in the central market serves classics such as cottage cheese with raisins and buttery crumbs or buckwheat and walnut, plus newer flavours like mango yoghurt and chocolate orange.

19. Get some stunning views of Ljubljana Castle from the Nebotičnik rooftop bar

The 13-storey Nebotičnik has a rooftop café with a wrap-around deck, and unlike the castle viewpoint, its panorama includes the castle itself. It serves drinks only, with cocktails at a fair 7–8€.

20. Visit the House of Illusions – one of the best things to do in Ljubljana on a rainy day

Optical illusions, distorting mirrors and a tilted room fill three floors and take about half an hour. Tickets are 12€, and it usually stays open until 9pm.

21. Find the creepy faces on Locksmith Street

Ključavničarska ulica, or Locksmith Street, is an easily missed alley marked by a key symbol. Its central gully holds 700 small bronze faces with different expressions, leading to a fountain and a caged skeleton.

They are the work of Slovenian sculptor Jakov Brdar, who also made the Butchers’ Bridge sculptures, and were inspired by a passage on people’s many faces in Rilke’s The Notebooks of Malte Laurids Brigge.

22. Discover Ljubljana’s growing craft beer scene

Beyond orange wine, craft beer is on the rise, from hoppy IPAs to sours. Bars to try:

  • Sax Pub – riverside seating and Augustiner Helles from Munich.
  • Pivnica Lajbah – a garden bar with the best local craft selection on tap seen in Slovenia.
  • Tektonik kraft pivovarna – a stylish spot visited several times on a recent stay nearby.

23. Take in the mixture of Art Nouveau and Brutalist architecture

Brutalist Republic Square, where Milan Kučan declared Slovenia’s independence on 26 June 1991, contrasts sharply with the Art Nouveau roof of Hauptmann’s House and the colourful Cooperative Bank headquarters nearby.

24. Walk around Metelkova, the alternative side of the city

Once a Yugoslav army barracks and squatted in the 1990s, Metelkova is now an autonomous arts quarter, similar to Copenhagen’s Christiania, covered in street art and home to small galleries. A daytime look takes about half an hour; keep people’s faces out of photos.

25. Explore the coolest area of town

Trubarjeva Cesta, a street back from the river, has an edgier feel with independent shops, bars and global food, including vintage store Textile House and Raw Pasta.

26. Sit at the bar where the river splits – one of the best things to do in Ljubljana on a sunny day

For a sunny riverside seat, sit outside Spica Café where the Ljubljanica divides; it is a local favourite with a great atmosphere.

27. Find the cyanometer

Fairly central stands a cyanometer, a sleek reflective column that judges air quality by how blue the sky is.

It photographs the sky, compares it with a colour wheel, changes its own colour to match and sends the data to the Environment Agency, all on solar power. It works almost like modern art, mirroring the city’s clean air.

28. Enjoy a great lunch at Mala Terasa

For something lighter than hearty local fare, Mala Terasa serves fresh, healthy lunches with good vegan and vegetarian options on a first-floor terrace. The changing daily menu costs under 10€, and the cyanometer is close by.

29. Go for homemade iced tea at Ferdinand

This locals’ bar opposite the Church of St Florian makes a different homemade iced tea every day, mixes good cocktails and lights the street with an outdoor chandelier after dark.

30. Visit the National Museum of Slovenia

Ideal for a rainy morning, it holds Roman bronzes, Egyptian mummies, a woolly mammoth and a 60,000-year-old Neanderthal flute said to be the oldest known instrument. The grand staircase with its sliding statues is a highlight; entry is 8€.

31. Visit the beautiful cathedral

Saint Nicholas’s Church, beside the market, is known for its green dome. Entry is free, and the gold-and-white interior and grand organ outshine the plain exterior. The bronze doors depict Slovenia’s Christian history, with a shiny patch people rub for luck.

32. Do some spectacular day trips from Ljubljana

With more than a few days, it is worth seeing more of Slovenia. Two trips work well by public transport:

  • Lake Bled – exceptionally photogenic and calm in October, with views of the island church and traditional cream cake. Take the train to Lesce-Bled (about an hour), then a 20-minute bus.
  • Postojna Cave – described as the world’s second biggest cave and very cold inside, about 90 minutes away by train. A shuttle continues to Predjama Castle, built into a cliff and tied to a local Robin Hood-style legend.

Things to do in Ljubljana, Slovenia – useful information for your trip

Where to stay in Ljubljana, Slovenia

Options worth a look, at prices noted when the guide was written:

  • Hotel Heritage – stylish design, about 130€ a night.
  • Petkovsek Rooms and Apartments – a pretty mid-range choice at about 100€.
  • Thomas Apartment 028 – about 80€, outside the centre but 10 minutes in on buses 6 and 14.
  • OH Apartments & Rooms – budget-friendly at about 60€, next to Tivoli Park.

How to get around Ljubljana, Slovenia

The compact centre is easy to walk, buses are cheap and reliable, and the well-connected station makes it simple to pair with Zagreb, Croatia.

When to go to Ljubljana, Slovenia

Autumn, especially October, brings comfortable walking weather, red ivy and golden colours. Spring also works, while summer is the busiest season.
