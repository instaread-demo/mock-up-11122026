What Is Structural Family Therapy Used For?

Emma-Marie Smith

Structural family therapy (SFT) is an approach to therapy based on systems theory. At its core is the belief that the ‘presenting problem' of an individual client is the result of unhelpful patterns of interaction within that person's family unit. This is referred to as their “inter-connected social system.”

The structural family therapist works not with the individual, but within the family, helping members to identify unhealthy patterns and to explore new ways of dealing with the challenges of family life. So, what is structural family therapy and what is it used for?

What Is Structural Family Therapy?

Psychiatrist Dr. Salvador Minuchin developed it in the 1960s, after work with troubled adolescents showed him the value of family involvement.

It examines hierarchy, subsystems, boundaries, power, alliances, coalitions, disengagement and enmeshment, and is a practical, popular family treatment.

Goals of Structural Family Therapy

It eases the immediate problem first, then aims for healthier long-term family patterns.

A “flexible structure” helps the family handle transitions like adolescence, illness, job loss or bereavement, for example by helping co-parents cooperate.

Structural Family Therapy Techniques

Its methods center on the present rather than on past history, and include:

Genogram: The therapist maps the family's hierarchy, boundaries, relationships and alliances.

Joining: The therapist builds an empathic bond, at times mirroring the family's language or behavior.

Enactment or role-play: A staged scene lets the family spot a problem interaction and try alternatives.

Boundary-making and restructuring: The family adopts clear boundaries and adjusts its hierarchy, perhaps via new seating or someone leaving the room.

Reframing: The therapist recasts a problem constructively, steering the family's story toward hope and self-esteem.

Sessions may happen at home, child-welfare agencies, hospitals or mental health clinics; therapists may ask to record them or use a one-way mirror.

Benefits of Structural Family Therapy

This goal-focused, “no blame” approach uses positive reframing and draws on members' own strengths to support lasting change.

It is often used for behavior problems in children, and may help with mental illness, addiction, eating disorders, grief or blended families.
