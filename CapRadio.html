Truckee guide company at center of deadly avalanche shuts down

Sarit Laschinsky
Wednesday, November 11, 2026 | Sacramento, CA

The Truckee-based guide company involved in February’s deadly avalanche near Perry’s Peak in the Sierra Nevada backcountry — the worst in modern California history — has ceased operations.

Blackbird Mountain Guides posted the update to its website, along with a short remembrance for the three company guides and six clients who perished in the Feb. 17 avalanche.

“Wherever we are in the mountains — every ridge, every quiet morning — the nine we lost are with us. In our hearts. Always.” the message reads.

Google lists the company as permanently closed, its social media accounts appear deleted, and it never reopened after halting bookings. CapRadio has asked Blackbird for comment.

Safety violations and fines

The closure follows Cal/OSHA’s decision to fine Blackbird more than $150,000 for seven alleged violations tied to the deaths of guides Andrew Alissandratos, Nicole Choo and Michael Henry. The largest penalty, $81,000, was rated “willful-serious accident related.”

In its last website post, on Aug. 21, Blackbird said it was appealing the citations and that it had complied with all applicable rules. Cal/OSHA still shows the violations as contested.

The citations came days after Blackbird released a preliminary investigation by veteran avalanche expert Don Sharaf.

That report found an open creek blocked the group’s pre-mapped route away from avalanche paths. Cal/OSHA’s Bureau of Investigations and the Nevada County Sheriff’s Office each opened their own investigations, and the sheriff later sent the case to the Nevada County District Attorney’s Office.

Blackbird’s history

Zebulon “Zeb” Blais started Blackbird in 2020, offering courses, ski trips and expeditions in California, Washington and abroad, including in bad weather. The American institute for Avalanche Research and Education recognized it for training the most students in the 2023-2024 season, and its three lost guides held AMGA certifications.

The final trip

The victims were among 15 people on a three-day trip to the Frog Lake Huts northwest of Truckee. They left Feb. 15 under an avalanche watch, which the Sierra Avalanche Center raised to a warning hours before the Feb. 17 slide.

Per Sharaf, the slide hit around 11 a.m. on the way back to the trailhead, burying 13; surviving guide Ryan Tilley and a client dug out four survivors. Nevada County Coroner’s Office records say all nine died of asphyxia, and all reportedly carried beacons.

On Feb. 21, the Sheriff’s Office released the victims’ names:

  • Andrew Alissandratos, 34, of Verdi, Nevada
  • Carrie Atkin, 46, of Soda Springs, California
  • Nicole Choo, 42, of South Lake Tahoe, California
  • Lizabeth “Liz” Clabaugh, 52, of Boise, Idaho
  • Michael Henry, 30, of Soda Springs, California
  • Danielle Keatley, 44, of Soda Springs and Larkspur, California
  • Kate Morse, 45, of Soda Springs and Tiburon, California
  • Caroline Sekar, 45, of Soda Springs and San Francisco, California
  • Katherine “Kate” Vitt, 43, of Greenbrae, California

Relatives said Atkin, Clabaugh, Keatley, Morse, Sekar and Vitt were part of an eight-person friend group of experienced skiers. Blackbird honored its three guides as skilled colleagues and friends.

Greg Micek contributed to this story.
